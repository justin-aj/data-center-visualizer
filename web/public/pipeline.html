How it works

The product is the pipeline. The 3D scene is its output.

Public development records are scattered across city filings, state permits and GIS servers. Our pipeline gathers them, checks them, labels every number with where it came from, and publishes a versioned package. Any viewer can render that package, and anyone can trace a building's height back to its source.

Built for trust

A civic visual is only useful if people can believe it. These rules are part of the pipeline's contract and are not optional.

Every value is labeled

Each height, size and position says how we know it.

OfficialDerivedEstimatedUnknown

Versioned, never silently changed

A corrected value means a new package version. The Lowell pilot is lowell-markley/0.1.0, reviewed and approved for context-only publication.

Neutral by design

The tool shows documented geometry. It does not rate the project, predict health effects or decide legal compliance.

Privacy first

Owner names, abutter names and contact details are removed before publication. Only data cleared for redistribution is included.

Why Lowell, why now

The Markley data center at the former Prince pasta plant is the subject of a permit dispute, a city moratorium and a court order. News reports describe residents, the company and the state arguing over equipment whose size and placement is hard to picture from paper plans.

Where it goes next

Data and display are separate, so growth is mostly new packages, not new app code.

1. Finish the Lowell proposal

Georeference the August 2024 plan set and model the generators and cooling towers from permit filings. Then residents can switch between today and the proposal.

2. Any Massachusetts town

MassGIS building, parcel and road layers cover the whole state, so the context stage can extend beyond Lowell.

3. Any large project

The same contract fits battery storage, warehouses, solar farms and housing developments that go before a planning board.

4. See it from your street

Enter an address and view the proposal from a nearby public sidewalk or park, with no address stored.

5. Real terrain and shadows

Add statewide LiDAR elevation and sun-angle shadows by date, so the scene shows grade and shading, not a flat plane.

6. Hearing-ready exports

Print-ready images with source labels, made for public comment letters, local reporting and planning-board slides.
